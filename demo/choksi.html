Prepared for Choksi Laboratories

One paneer and water request, from the client's email to a verified report

We set up a demo lab group with two sites, a central lab in Indore and a branch in Vadodara, loaded a few paneer and packaged drinking water tests, and ran one client request through LabTessa from start to finish. These are the screens your team would use. All lab names, clients and results here are sample data.

1Each site is its own lab, under one login

Staff who work at more than one site sign in once and choose the site they are working in. Each site keeps its own samples, clients, accredited scope and reports, and its site code goes into every sample code, so a sample from Indore (IDR) can never be mixed up with one from Vadodara (VAD).

2The client's request arrives by email

A typical request from a food and beverage client:

From: Neha Jain, QA Manager, Narmada Foods & Beverages (sample client)Dear team, We have dispatched two samples today from our Dewas plant: 1) Malai paneer, batch MP-1142, one 1 kg pack. Please test moisture and milk fat on dry matter as per FSSAI. 2) Packaged drinking water, batch PW-0915, one carton of 1 L bottles. Please test pH, TDS, nitrate and lead. Kindly share the NABL report by Monday.

Instead of typing it in, the receptionist pastes the email, or attaches the PDF or .eml, and ticks Propose samples from the documents.

3LabTessa drafts the samples, and a person confirms them

A few seconds later both samples are drafted, matched to the Indore site's own test list: the matrix, batch number, quantity and tests. Every field shows the client's exact words it came from, highlighted. Nothing is registered until the receptionist checks it and clicks confirm.

The client also asked for lead in water. In this demo, lead in water is not in the Indore site's accredited scope, so LabTessa flags it and leaves it unticked. Your team decides whether to accept it as a non-accredited test or send it to the site that holds that scope.

4Results are checked against your limits at once

When the samples arrive, receipt is recorded and each one gets its site-coded lab number (IDR-2026-000001, IDR-2026-000002). The analyst enters results, and each result is judged straight away against the limit your lab entered, with its source shown. Every result then waits for approval by a second person: whoever entered a result can't approve it.

5The test report, ready to sign

After approval, the reviewer issues the report with an authorised signatory. It shows the method, result, limit and source for every test, and carries a QR code. Once issued, the PDF can't be changed; a correction becomes a revised report that says which one it replaces.

6Anyone can check that a report is genuine

A client, a buyer or a regulator who scans the QR code sees whether the report was issued by your lab and is the current version. They don't see the client's name or the results, so the code is safe to print on packaging or share with buyers.
